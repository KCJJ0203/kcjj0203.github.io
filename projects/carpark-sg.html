Case study

Carpark SG

A live map of about 2,970 carparks in Singapore. It tells you what time to park for the lowest fee, not just where to park.

01

The problem

Singapore already has good parking apps, like wheretopark.sg. They answer one question well: which carpark near me has a space?

None of them answer a different question: what hour should I come? Rates here change at 8.30am, 5pm and 10.30pm, and again on Sundays and public holidays. Two hours on the same Orchard street costs $4.80 at lunchtime and nothing after 5pm. A list sorted by distance or price only shows one moment, so that saving never shows up.

02

The idea: when, not where

So the front page gives a verdict about time first, and the carparks come second. Search a place, say how long you are staying, and it tells you something like this:

Arrive after 5.00pm and pay free.It is $2.40 arriving 1.00pm.

Under it is a strip of 24 bars, one for each hour, showing the cheapest parking near the centre of the map if you arrive then. Tap an hour and the verdict, the list and every price pin re-price for that time. Past hours are dimmed, so it never suggests a time that is already gone.

03

How it works

  • Three open data sources. Every HDB carpark, every URA carpark and street bay, and 40 malls from LTA.
  • One adapter per source. Adding a source is one adapter file plus one line in a list. Every id carries its source, like hdb:ACB, ura:A0007 or lta:23, so feeds never clash.
  • Prices from the source. URA ships rates with each carpark. HDB only lists its rates on a web page, so they are typed in with the date they were read, and a weekly check flags any change. LTA publishes no prices, so malls show free lots only.
  • A scheduled collector. It saves a snapshot of live availability every 30 minutes. GitHub Actions rebuilds and publishes the site data once a day. The page itself is static, with no accounts and no tracking.
  • Predictions from history. Where HDB charges the same rate all day, the strip shows how full nearby carparks usually are at each hour instead, using the history of close to 2,000 HDB carparks.

04

What was hard

  • Coordinates. The government data uses SVY21, Singapore's own map grid. Read as latitude and longitude, every carpark lands off the coast of West Africa. The converter is checked against OneMap and agrees to within 8 centimetres.
  • A wrong number is worse than no number. A carpark reporting 0 lots shows as not reporting, not as full. A big carpark with every lot free usually has dead sensors, so it is flagged and gets no predictions. Where URA publishes no rate, the app says so instead of guessing free.
  • Gaps in the data. The first collector ran on my desktop and got 6 or 7 snapshots a day instead of 48, because the PC went to sleep. Later, GitHub's scheduler quietly fell to 3 a day while every run still passed. Now more than one scheduler feeds the history, and a script reports the gaps for each day.

05

What I learned

  • Test the data before the idea. My earlier project compared supermarket prices. It died because the shops blocked collection or had no online catalogue, and I only found out after the matching engine was built. This time I checked first: 2,007 of 2,015 live carparks matched their location records before any app code was written.
  • Check what you collected, not just that it ran. A job can finish without errors every time and still leave half the day empty.
  • Answer a different question. wheretopark.sg already wins at finding a carpark near you, so I competed on timing and price instead.
  • Directing an AI coding agent is real work. I planned each feature, directed the agent and tested every release myself. My rule now: use the app like a real driver before trusting any description of what it does.
